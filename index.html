Skipping the work

Picture a board game that never ends. On every square you pick a move, and each move takes you to another square and pays you some points. Since you play forever, what counts is your average score per move. From each square, what is the best average you can get?

You always end up going round some loop again and again, so the answer comes down to finding the loops with the best average. This page shows the standard way to find them, and our idea: a cheap test that proves whole parts of the board aren't worth searching.

Why it matters ↓

Why it matters

The “board” is a deterministic Markov decision process (DMDP), and the best average is its gain. The same loop-finding problem turns up well outside games:

Chips

In a digital circuit, the loop with the longest average delay sets how fast the clock can tick. The benchmark graphs we test on are real circuits.

Pipelines

In a production line or a program that repeats, the slowest repeating cycle caps how much gets done per hour.

Games

Mean-payoff games, used to design systems that run forever and keep behaving well, are the two-player version of the same question.
